\begin{abstract}
Graph neural networks are usually justified by a homophily assumption -- linked nodes tend to
share labels -- but the assumption is left implicit inside the architecture, so the value of
stating it explicitly is rarely measured. We hold one backbone fixed and impose the same
assumption three ways of increasing probabilistic seriousness: a Laplacian penalty on the
outputs, an expected rule-violation (semantic) loss, and an explicit Potts Markov random field
over labels whose unary potentials are the frozen network's logits, solved by convergent
mean-field inference. Under a pre-registered protocol (7{,}744 runs, 10--30 paired seeds) we
cross label scarcity, prior correctness on a contextual stochastic block model, and the
mechanism of imposition. Three findings emerge, two of which contradict the standard framing.
First, our pre-registered scarcity hypothesis is \emph{refuted and significantly reversed}: the
prior's benefit grows with supervision ($-7.07$ points, 95\% CI $[-10.26,-3.91]$), because
relational inference propagates label information rather than creating it. Second, the harm
from a misspecified prior appears \emph{only} when its strength is fixed in advance: a dogmatic
prior loses up to 45 points through posterior collapse, while a validation-tuned prior never
loses more than 0.5. Third, a degree-preserving rewiring control shows the popular Laplacian
penalty retains most of its benefit on a graph with no label structure, whereas the MRF's
benefit vanishes -- the former is largely generic regularization, not relational reasoning.
All inference is validated against exact marginals on 11{,}280 comparisons; at the couplings
the models actually use on Cora, Gibbs sampling fails to mix, which we report rather than
conceal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Node classification is the canonical setting in which observations are not independent: a
paper's topic is predictable from the topics of the papers it cites. Graph neural networks
exploit this by smoothing representations over edges, and they are typically motivated by
\emph{homophily} -- the assumption that an edge signals a shared label. Labels are also
expensive, so the practically interesting regime is one where only a handful of nodes per class
are annotated, and prior knowledge should matter most.

This motivates a natural proposal, and the one this project began from: write the homophily
assumption down explicitly as a differentiable penalty and add it to the training objective.
The difficulty is that the comparison is confounded. A graph convolution \emph{is} Laplacian
smoothing \citep{li2018deeper}, so a GCN baseline already contains the assumption; measuring
whether an explicit penalty raises its accuracy conflates ``does the prior help?'' with ``does
this backbone already exploit it?''. Worse, a null result would be uninterpretable, and a
positive one would not tell us whether the prior worked \emph{because} it is relational.

We therefore ask a sharper question with three axes: \textbf{when} does an explicit homophily
prior help, as a function of label scarcity and of how well the prior matches the graph, and
does it matter \textbf{how} the prior is imposed -- at training time on the parameters, or at
inference time on the posterior of an explicit graphical model? A fourth, methodological
question runs underneath: is the approximate inference faithful enough that the answers are
about the model rather than the algorithm?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item A pre-registered, matched-budget factorial study crossing label scarcity, prior
correctness, and imposition mechanism on a fixed backbone, with three confirmatory endpoints
fixed before any experiment ran. One is refuted in the direction opposite to the prediction,
and we report it as such.
\item A separation of \emph{relational reasoning} from \emph{generic regularization} using a
degree-preserving rewiring control, explained by an algebraic identity (Proposition~1) showing
what the Laplacian penalty optimizes beyond edgewise agreement.
\item An inference-validation layer: mean-field, loopy belief propagation and chromatic Gibbs
scored against exact marginals on 11{,}280 controlled comparisons, including the honest
negative that no trustworthy MCMC reference exists at the couplings the models select.
\end{itemize}

We claim none of the ingredients as new. Homophily as a probabilistic prior with clamped labels
is \citet{zhu2003semi}; logic rules as differentiable regularizers are \citet{hu2016harnessing}
and the semantic loss of \citet{xu2018semantic}; GNN--CRF hybrids trained with EM are GMNN
\citep{qu2019gmnn}; post-hoc propagation on a base predictor is APPNP
\citep{gasteiger2019predict} and Correct \& Smooth \citep{huang2021combining}; heterophily as a
failure regime is \citet{zhu2020beyond} and \citet{ma2022homophily}. The contribution is the
controlled \emph{measurement} under matched conditions.

\section{Background and related work}

\paragraph{Graph convolution as smoothing.} A two-layer GCN \citep{kipf2017semi} propagates
$H^{(\ell+1)}=\sigma(\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}H^{(\ell)}W^{(\ell)})$. This is
Laplacian smoothing of features \citep{li2018deeper}: the homophily prior is already present,
implicitly, in the architecture. Any measured benefit of an explicit prior is therefore a
\emph{marginal} effect over that implicit one -- which is why we also apply the explicit prior
to a graph-blind MLP, where no such double counting occurs.

\paragraph{Graph-based semi-supervised learning.} Treating labels as a random field with
observed labels clamped and the rest inferred is the harmonic-function method of
\citet{zhu2003semi}. Modern decoupled methods revisit it: APPNP \citep{gasteiger2019predict}
diffuses a base predictor's outputs, and Correct \& Smooth \citep{huang2021combining} shows
post-hoc propagation on a simple predictor rivals end-to-end GNNs. Analytically, label
propagation and graph convolution can be derived as conditional expectations under one Gaussian
MRF \citep{jia2022unifying, wang2020unifying}, and GNN message passing itself mimics mean-field
and BP updates \citep{dai2016discriminative}.

\paragraph{Homophily and its measurement.} Raw edge homophily is not comparable across graphs;
adjusted homophily and label informativeness are better behaved \citep{platonov2023characterizing}.
Low homophily does not imply an uninformative graph -- ``good heterophily'' exists when
neighbour label distributions are predictive \citep{ma2022homophily} -- a distinction our
synthetic experiment makes concrete. Benchmarks and evaluation protocols under heterophily are
themselves unreliable \citep{platonov2023critical}, and single-split Planetoid evaluation is
known to flip conclusions \citep{shchur2018pitfalls}, motivating our multi-seed paired protocol.

\paragraph{Approximate inference.} Benchmarking loopy BP against exact marginals on tractable
networks follows \citet{murphy1999loopy}; our chromatic Gibbs schedule follows
\citet{gonzalez2011parallel}. Our synthetic generator is a contextual stochastic block model
\citep{deshpande2018contextual}.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Setup}

We are given a graph $G=(V,E)$ with features $X$ and labels $y_i\in\{1,\dots,C\}$ observed on a
small training set. All experiments are transductive: features and edges of validation and test
nodes are visible during training, labels are not. An encoder $f_\theta$ (an MLP that ignores
$G$, or a GCN that uses it) produces logits $s_i(c)$, and $p_i=\mathrm{softmax}(s_i)$.

\subsection{One assumption, three impositions}

Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} shows the design. With edgewise agreement
$A(p)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E} p_i^\top p_j$, the three rungs are:

\textbf{(M2) Laplacian penalty} -- the original proposal, a smoothness heuristic on the outputs:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm lap}=\tfrac{1}{|E|}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\|p_i-p_j\|_2^2,
\qquad \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm CE}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\rm lap}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{(M3) Expected rule violation} -- under the factorized approximation,
$\Pr(y_i=y_j)=p_i^\top p_j$, so the probability that the rule
$\textsc{Edge}(i,j)\Rightarrow\textsc{SameClass}(i,j)$ fails is a semantic loss
\citep{xu2018semantic}: $\mathcal{L}_{\rm rule}=\tfrac{1}{|E|}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(1-p_i^\top p_j)$.

\textbf{(M4) Explicit Potts MRF} -- the assumption as a joint distribution over labels, with the
\emph{frozen} logits as unary potentials:
\begin{equation}
p(y\mid X,G)\;\propto\;\exp\Big[\sum_i s_i(y_i)+\beta\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\!\!w_{ij}\,
\mathbf{1}[y_i=y_j]\Big].
\label{eq:potts}
\end{equation}
Inference is \emph{post-hoc}: the encoder is trained normally, then Eq.~\ref{eq:potts} is solved.
This is the isolation that makes the comparison clean -- every MRF variant is a pure function of
the same unary evidence, so differences are attributable to the prior and the inference, not to
a different fit.

\subsection{Proposition 1: what the three mechanisms share, and where they differ}

\begin{quote}
With $A(p)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}p_i^\top p_j$,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\rm rule}=1-\tfrac{A(p)}{|E|},\qquad
\mathcal{L}_{\rm lap}=\tfrac{1}{|E|}\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\!\!\big(\|p_i\|^2+\|p_j\|^2\big)-\tfrac{2A(p)}{|E|},
\]
\[
\mathcal{F}_{\rm MF}(q)=\sum_i q_i^\top s_i+\beta A(q)+\sum_i H(q_i).
\]
\end{quote}
\emph{Proof:} expand $\|p_i-p_j\|^2=\|p_i\|^2+\|p_j\|^2-2p_i^\top p_j$ and sum; the mean-field
objective is the standard ELBO for Eq.~\ref{eq:potts} under $q(y)=\prod_i q_i(y_i)$. $\square$

All three are driven by the \emph{same} pairwise statistic $A$. They differ in (i) which
variable is optimized -- network parameters at training time versus the posterior at test time --
and (ii) the companion term: the Laplacian form carries an extra \textbf{confidence penalty}
$\sum_i \deg_i\|p_i\|^2$ that discourages confident predictions regardless of what the graph
says, whereas the mean-field objective carries an \textbf{entropy} term. Section~\ref{sec:mech}
tests this prediction directly.

\subsection{Inference}

The mean-field update for Eq.~\ref{eq:potts} is
\begin{equation}
q_i(c)\;\propto\;\exp\Big[s_i(c)+\beta\sum_{j\in N(i)}w_{ij}\,q_j(c)\Big],
\label{eq:mf}
\end{equation}
which we run \emph{to convergence} (never as a tuned iteration count) using colour-blocked
coordinate ascent: nodes sharing a graph colour share no edge, so a whole colour class updates
at once and the update remains exact block coordinate ascent, so the ELBO is monotone (asserted
at every block). Note Eq.~\ref{eq:mf} is APPNP-style propagation with the unary evidence
re-injected each sweep, but over the raw adjacency rather than the symmetric normalization a
GCN applies -- the two therefore smooth differently.

We additionally implement damped loopy BP (exploiting the Potts structure for $O(C)$ messages)
and chromatic Gibbs sampling with Rao-Blackwellized marginals \citep{gonzalez2011parallel},
gated by split $\hat{R}$ and ESS. Exact marginals come from brute-force enumeration
($C^n\le 5\times10^6$) or repeated min-fill variable elimination.

\subsection{Two prior regimes}

A subtlety decides whether harm is observable at all. If $\beta$ is selected on validation, the
baseline is nested at $\beta\to 0$, so a wrong prior is simply tuned away and no harm can
appear. We therefore pre-register two regimes: \textbf{tuned} ($\beta$ chosen per condition on
validation -- the prior as an \emph{option}) and \textbf{dogmatic} ($\beta$ fixed at the value
selected in a reference homophilous cell and applied everywhere -- the prior as an
\emph{assumption}).

\section{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.} Cora and CiteSeer \citep{sen2008collective, yang2016revisiting} via the
standard Planetoid split, plus a contextual SBM \citep{deshpande2018contextual} in which edge
homophily $h$ is manipulated while expected degree, class balance, feature dimension and feature
noise are held fixed, using $p_{\rm in}=hk/(n_c-1)$, $p_{\rm out}=(1-h)k/(n-n_c)$. Because the
generative process is known it also yields a closed-form feature-only Bayes oracle
(\textsc{Oracle-F}) and the generator-implied coupling
$\beta_{\rm gen}=\log(p_{\rm in}/p_{\rm out})$ used by \textsc{Oracle-G}. Table~\ref{tab:data}
reports statistics recomputed from the loaded objects.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Pre-registered before any run and frozen under a git tag: 10 paired seeds
(30 on confirmatory cells), $m\in\{1,2,5,10,20\}$ labels/class sampled stratified and nested
across $m$; backbone hyperparameters tuned once per dataset \emph{and per architecture} at $m=5$
and then frozen; prior strength tuned per condition on validation with an equal budget of six
configurations; early stopping on validation accuracy. Selection is a pure function of
validation accuracy and never reads a test column. The CSBM's feature noise $\sigma_x$ was fixed
once on quarantined pilot seeds by requiring \textsc{Oracle-F} accuracy in $[0.70,0.80]$, and
never retuned.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Every comparison is paired by seed. We report the mean paired
difference, a 95\% BCa bootstrap interval ($B=10{,}000$) and the sign count. Only three
endpoints, fixed in advance, receive a hypothesis test (Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank);
everything else is estimation and is labelled exploratory. On the real graphs the seeds vary the
training sample and initialization on a \emph{fixed} graph and test set, so intervals describe
protocol variability, not generalization to new graphs; only the CSBM redraws the graph per seed.

\paragraph{Scale and validation.} 7{,}744 model runs and 11{,}280 exact-inference comparisons,
CPU only. Before any experiment, gates verified that $\lambda=0$ reproduces the plain GCN to
$<10^{-5}$, that $\beta=0$ reproduces $\mathrm{softmax}(s)$ to $2.1\times10^{-14}$ for every
engine, that enumeration and variable elimination agree to $<10^{-10}$, and that permuting test
labels leaves training output bit-identical. Thirteen automated audit checks pass.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The prior's value tracks unary quality, not label scarcity}

Our first pre-registered endpoint (C1) predicted that the MRF's benefit over a feature-only MLP
would be larger at $m{=}2$ than at $m{=}20$. Over 30 paired seeds the contrast is
$\theta=-7.07$ points, 95\% CI $[-10.26,-3.91]$, $p=6.6\times10^{-4}$, 7/30 seeds positive:
\textbf{the hypothesis is refuted, and the effect is significantly reversed}.

Figure~\ref{fig:lowlabel}(b) shows why this is not a mundane null. The two arms move in
opposite directions. Measured against the GCN, the explicit prior's benefit \emph{shrinks} with
supervision ($+4.38\to+0.59$ points for the Laplacian penalty from $m{=}1$ to $m{=}20$).
Measured against the MLP, it \emph{grows} ($+4.45\to+19.55$).

The mechanism is visible in the selected couplings. At $m{=}1$ the MLP reaches $29.2\%$ against
a chance level of $14.3\%$: there is almost nothing to propagate, and validation selects a weak
coupling (median $\beta^\star=0.375$). From $m{\ge}2$ the unaries carry real signal, validation
saturates the grid ($\beta^\star=2.0$), and propagation converts feature evidence into a 19--22
point gain. Relational inference \emph{amplifies} label information already present in the
unary evidence; it does not create it. Against a GCN the pattern inverts because the GCN has
already propagated -- what is left for the explicit prior shrinks as supervision grows.
CiteSeer reproduces this independently ($+5.34$, $+12.46$, $+10.09$ points at
$m=1,5,20$; 10/10 seeds each), so the reversal is not a Cora artifact.

\subsection{Prior correctness governs benefit -- but only a committed prior can be harmed}

The CSBM sweep manipulates the truth of the prior directly. Both remaining endpoints are
supported: benefit grows with homophily (C2: $+78.77$ points, CI $[+55.53,+84.87]$, 10/10
seeds) and the dogmatic prior actively harms at $h{=}0.05$ (C3: $-29.72$ points, CI
$[-31.81,-28.16]$, 10/10 seeds). Both survive Holm correction.

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} makes the decisive comparison. Under the \textbf{dogmatic} regime the
same prior swings from $-45.2$ to $+54.7$ points; under the \textbf{tuned} regime the worst cell
is $-0.5$. The failure is not gradual degradation but \textbf{posterior collapse}: at
$\beta{=}2$, $h{=}0.05$ the argmax edge agreement reaches $0.966$ and accuracy falls to
$16.9\%\approx$ chance (Figure~\ref{fig:sens}c) -- the coupling term overwhelms the unaries and
nearly every node receives the same label. Because $\beta\to0$ nests the baseline, validation
simply retreats when the prior is wrong. \emph{Tuning is what converts a dangerous assumption
into a safe option}, and a study that tuned everywhere would have observed a benign heatmap and
concluded, wrongly, that the prior is harmless.

Per-seed interpolation of the zero crossing gives $h^\star = 0.76\,[0.52,0.87]$ at $m{=}1$,
$0.57\,[0.40,0.59]$ at $m{=}5$ and $0.43\,[0.42,0.43]$ at $m{=}20$ (10/10 seeds;
Figure~\ref{fig:cross}a): more supervision lets a model tolerate a wronger prior.

\paragraph{A low-homophily graph is not an uninformative graph.} At $h{=}0.05$ the generator
implies a \emph{negative} coupling, $\beta_{\rm gen}=-1.15$. \textsc{Oracle-G}, which uses the
true generative unaries with that coupling, reaches $77.0\%$ -- \emph{better} than the
feature-only \textsc{Oracle-F} ($73.4\%$) -- while the positive-$\beta$ Potts prior collapses to
$15\%$. The relational evidence is genuinely useful there; what fails is the \emph{same-label}
assumption, which has the wrong sign. This is direct, controlled evidence for the distinction
that \citet{ma2022homophily} draw between misspecification and uninformativeness. Validation
tuning cannot repair it: $\beta^\star$ saturates at the grid maximum while $\beta_{\rm gen}$
rises to $3.99$ at $h{=}0.9$, and no positive $\beta$ can express an anti-homophilous
compatibility at all (Figure~\ref{fig:cross}b).

\subsection{The Laplacian penalty is largely generic regularization; the MRF is relational}
\label{sec:mech}

Proposition~1 predicts that the Laplacian penalty does something a relational prior should not:
it penalizes confidence irrespective of the graph. We test this with a control rather than an
accuracy comparison. Rewiring Cora with a degree-preserving configuration model destroys label
structure while preserving every node's degree (edge homophily falls from $0.810$ to
$0.168$). On the rewired graph:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Model on \emph{rewired} Cora & $\Delta$ vs GCN (pts) & 95\% CI & seeds $+$ \\
\midrule
GCN + Laplacian penalty, $m{=}2$ & $+3.24$ & $[+1.82,+5.17]$ & 9/10 \\
MRF on GCN (mean-field), $m{=}2$ & $+0.11$ & $[-0.15,+0.74]$ & 4/10 \\
GCN + Laplacian penalty, $m{=}5$ & $+2.22$ & $[+0.52,+4.40]$ & 7/10 \\
MRF on GCN (mean-field), $m{=}5$ & $-0.06$ & $[-0.23,+0.15]$ & 3/10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The MRF's benefit \textbf{vanishes} -- its interval covers zero at both label rates -- so it is
genuinely relational. The Laplacian penalty's benefit \textbf{survives} on a graph whose edges
carry no label information, with intervals excluding zero. Much of what the original proposal's
regularizer contributes on Cora is therefore not relational reasoning at all, but the confidence
penalty that Proposition~1 exposes.

\paragraph{Two honest caveats.} First, a three-layer GCN -- pure extra propagation, no
probabilistic machinery -- gains $+2.91$ and $+3.69$ points at $m{=}1,2$, comparable to the
explicit prior in that regime; we therefore claim no advantage over deeper propagation at very
low label rates. Second, mechanism claims are only made above a measured noise floor: two GCNs
differing \emph{only} by initialization seed already disagree on $16.2\%$ ($m{=}2$), $14.2\%$
($m{=}5$) and $6.5\%$ ($m{=}20$) of test predictions.

\subsection{Consistency is not accuracy, and confident is not calibrated}

Rule satisfaction $R=\frac{1}{|E|}\sum p_i^\top p_j$ and argmax edge agreement increase
monotonically with $\beta$ while accuracy peaks and then collapses
(Figure~\ref{fig:sens}). At $h{=}0.05,\beta{=}2$ the model is maximally self-consistent
($0.966$) and minimally correct ($16.9\%$). Optimizing agreement with a symbolic rule is thus
not a proxy for optimizing correctness -- a caution that applies directly to the neuro-symbolic
framing this project started from.

The same warning appears in the probabilities. At $m{=}2$ on Cora the MRF improves MLP accuracy
by $+11.2$ points yet \emph{worsens} test NLL from $1.86$ to $2.86$: strong coupling produces
confident, sometimes confidently wrong, marginals. Only once supervision is ample does the
probability quality improve as well (at $m{=}20$, MRF(GCN) NLL $0.978$ vs GCN $1.055$).

\subsection{The inference is validated, and where it fails it fails diagnosably}

Because every conclusion above rests on approximate marginals, we score all three engines
against exact inference on graphs small enough to solve exactly (Figure~\ref{fig:fid}).
Loopy BP is exact on all three tree structures at every coupling (max TV $4.8\times10^{-7}$, at
the message-tolerance floor), and every engine reproduces $\mathrm{softmax}(s)$ at $\beta=0$ to
$2.1\times10^{-14}$ -- the gates that license trusting the implementation. Mean-field error
grows steeply with coupling on every structure (TV $0.19$--$0.48$ at $\beta=4$): its
factorized family cannot represent the dependence that strong coupling creates. The bias/variance
split is explicit: on the $4\times4$ grid at $\beta=1.75$, Gibbs error falls from $0.077$ to
$0.007$ as kept sweeps grow from 10 to 3{,}000, while mean-field ($0.176$) and loopy BP
($0.060$) sit at fixed floors no extra compute removes.

\textbf{At scale the honest result is a diagnostic failure, not a comparison.} On Cora at
$m{=}5$ we ran gated Gibbs against converged mean-field on identical logits. Where Gibbs passed
its $\hat{R}$/ESS gates ($\beta\le1$) the two agreed closely (mean marginal TV $0.035$). Where
validation had selected $\beta=2$, Gibbs \emph{failed to mix} -- $\hat{R}$ up to $2.58$ and ESS
as low as $4.7$ from 8{,}000 samples -- so at exactly the coupling the models use, no
trustworthy reference exists. We report that rather than presenting either method as ground
truth: the Potts posterior on Cora at $\beta=2$ is multimodal enough to defeat a chromatic Gibbs
sampler at this budget.

\subsection{Robustness}

Re-selecting hyperparameters with only 5 labels/class of validation data -- rather than the
$\approx$500 the standard split provides -- preserves the effect ($+5.64$ points at $m{=}1$,
8/10 seeds; $+7.82$ at $m{=}2$, 9/10), so the conclusions do not depend on an unrealistically
large tuning set. Validation selects the $\beta$-grid maximum in most MRF-on-MLP cells, so the
reported gains are \emph{lower bounds}; an exploratory extension to $\beta\le8$ is reported in
the appendix and labelled as such.

\section{Discussion and future work}

\paragraph{What governs the value of a relational prior.} Our results replace the intuition
``priors matter when data is scarce'' with a more precise statement: a relational prior pays in
proportion to the relational information the base model has \emph{not} already extracted, and it
requires enough unary signal to propagate. Both conditions failed simultaneously in the regime
the original proposal targeted -- against a GCN, at one label per class -- which is exactly why
that comparison is the least informative one to run.

\paragraph{Why the mechanism distinction matters.} The rewiring control shows that a widely used
``logic'' regularizer is doing something other than relational reasoning. This is not an argument
against the penalty -- it helps -- but against the interpretation. If a symbolic penalty's
benefit survives the destruction of the structure the symbol refers to, the symbolic reading is
unsupported. We would encourage this control in any neuro-symbolic paper that claims a prior
encodes relational knowledge.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Ten seeds (30 on endpoints) give approximate interval coverage. On the
real graphs the intervals describe protocol variability on a fixed graph and test set, not
cross-graph generalization; only the CSBM has graph-level replication. We study one backbone,
one pairwise prior family and the transductive setting. The CSBM's magnitudes are
generator-conditional, since $\sigma_x$ is a designer-chosen effect-size dial, and at high $h$
the Potts prior is correct \emph{by construction} -- the SBM's edge log-odds are exactly the
Potts sufficient statistic -- so that sweep is a known-truth check of the measurement
instrument, not evidence about citation networks. The real-data results carry the
generalization claims alone.

\paragraph{Future work.} The wrong-sign failure at low homophily points directly at the fix:
replace the identity compatibility $\mathbf{1}[y_i=y_j]$ with a learned $C\times C$ matrix
$\psi(a,b)=\exp(\beta C_{ab})$ fitted by EM over the unobserved labels, which can represent
anti-homophily and which \textsc{Oracle-G} shows would be worth up to 62 points at $h{=}0.05$.
This is the GMNN direction \citep{qu2019gmnn}, now motivated by a measured deficiency rather
than by novelty. Second, the $\beta\ge2$ regime where Gibbs fails to mix calls for annealed or
structured inference. Third, richer rules than a single pairwise predicate would test whether
the confidence-penalty confound we identified generalizes.

\section{Conclusion}

A homophily prior is most useful when the base model has not already exploited the graph and the
unary evidence is informative enough to propagate; it becomes harmful when its strength is fixed
in advance on a graph that violates it, costing up to 45 points through posterior collapse below
$h^\star\approx0.57$ at $m{=}5$. The controlled experiments support this because the contextual
SBM manipulates the prior's correctness at fixed degree and features, the rewiring control
separates relational reasoning from generic regularization, and the exact-inference suite
certifies that these conclusions reflect the model rather than the approximation used to fit it.

\subsection{Protocol amendments}

\textbf{A1.} The pre-registered Gibbs convergence gate included a maximum inter-chain marginal
TV criterion. Implementation testing showed this statistic measures finite-budget Monte-Carlo
precision rather than mixing: a 12-node cycle at $\beta{=}1$ reached $\hat{R}=1.0004$ and
ESS $=5540$ -- textbook good mixing -- yet inter-chain TV $=0.0258$. Retaining it as a gate
would have mislabelled well-mixed chains as failures. It is still computed and reported; the
pass/fail decision uses $\hat{R}$ and ESS. Amended before any experimental result existed.

\textbf{A2.} The $\beta$ grid maximum is binding in most MRF-on-MLP cells. An extension to
$\beta\in\{3,4,6,8\}$ was run post-freeze and is labelled exploratory throughout.
